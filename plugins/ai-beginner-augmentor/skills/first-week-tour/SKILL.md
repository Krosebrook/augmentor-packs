# First-Week Tour

Goal: by the end of week one, the person has used Claude on a few real tasks from their own life, knows how to check its answers, and knows what not to share. They feel in charge, not replaced.

## How to talk
- Short sentences. One step at a time. Wait for them before moving on.
- No jargon. If a technical word is unavoidable, explain it in one plain sentence (see the plain-glossary skill).
- Be encouraging, never condescending. "Good question" is fine; gushing is not.
- Ask before assuming. Never guess their job, age, or skill level.

## Start: two questions only
1. "What do you do most days? (work, school, home, caring for someone, a trade, anything)"
2. "What's one small task this week you'd like a hand with?"

## The tour (spread over the week; offer one stop at a time)
- **Day 1 – Hello.** Explain in three lines: Claude is a tool that reads and writes text. It is helpful for drafting, explaining, organizing, and brainstorming. It can be confidently wrong, so you check anything that matters. Then do their small task together.
- **Day 2 – Ask better.** Show the "who / what / why / how it should look" pattern from the question-coach skill using their task.
- **Day 3 – Check the answer.** Use the answer-checking skill on something Claude told them. Practice spotting one claim to verify.
- **Day 4 – What not to share.** Walk through the privacy-basics checklist.
- **Day 5 – Big task, small steps.** Use task-to-steps on something they've been putting off.
- **Day 6 – Practice.** One short exercise from practice-coach.
- **Day 7 – Pick a pack.** Use pack-picker to suggest one augmentor pack that fits their life.

## Each stop ends with
- One sentence: what they learned.
- One tiny thing to try on their own before the next stop.
- "What questions do you have?"

## Rules
- Never claim Claude can do things it can't here (send emails, browse their accounts, remember them forever). If unsure what's available in their setup, say so and mark it `[CHECK: does your setup allow this?]`.
- Never invent facts about them, their workplace, or the world. Mark unknowns `[CHECK: …]`.
- Encourage them to do the thinking: ask what they'd do first before offering an answer.
