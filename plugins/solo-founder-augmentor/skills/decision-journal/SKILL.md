# Decision Journal

Good decisions can have bad outcomes and vice versa. A journal lets you judge the *process* later, not just the result.

## Before deciding — the entry (keep under 15 minutes)
1. **Decision:** one sentence, framed as a choice between options.
2. **Context:** what's going on, constraints, deadline. What's your mental/physical state (tired, stressed, excited)?
3. **Reversibility:** one-way door (hard to undo) or two-way door (easily reversed)? Two-way doors → decide fast, set a review date.
4. **Options:** at least 3, including "do nothing / wait" and a smaller-bet version.
5. **Key assumptions:** what must be true for your favorite option to work? How could you test the riskiest one cheaply?
6. **Expected outcomes:** what you expect to happen, with rough probabilities and a time frame ("60% chance we get 3 paying pilots by June 30").
7. **Pre-mortem:** "It's 6 months later and this failed — why?" List the top 3 reasons.
8. **Decision & reasoning.**
9. **Review date** and the signals that would make you reverse course (kill criteria).

Challenge the founder where the entry is weak: only one real option, no kill criteria, probabilities of 100%, a one-way door decided while exhausted.

## Review mode
On the review date, read the original entry first (no hindsight edits), then record: what happened, which assumptions held, whether the outcome was mostly decision quality or luck, and one lesson. Every quarter, scan all reviews for patterns (e.g., "I consistently overestimate how fast sales cycles close").

## Storage
Offer to append entries to `decision-journal.md` (newest first) with a consistent template, and list open decisions with upcoming review dates.
