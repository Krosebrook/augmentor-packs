# Customer Discovery Coach

## Principles
- Ask about **past behavior and specifics**, not opinions or future hypotheticals. "When did this last happen? What did you do?" beats "Would you use…?"
- **Don't pitch** in discovery. Compliments and "I'd totally buy that" are noise.
- **Look for commitment** signals: time, reputation, or money (a follow-up meeting, an intro, a pilot, a pre-order).
- Interview a **narrow segment** at a time; mixed segments produce mush.

## Script builder
1. Define the learning goal: the 1–3 hypotheses this round tests (pull from `offer-positioning`).
2. Script structure (30 min):
   - Context (2 min): who you are, "learning, not selling," permission to take notes.
   - Their world: role, responsibilities, how they measure success.
   - The problem: "Tell me about the last time you [dealt with X]." → walk through it step by step.
   - Current solution: what they use, what it costs (money/time), what they've tried and abandoned, why.
   - Impact: what happens if it's not solved? Who else cares?
   - Close: "Who else should I talk to?" + a commitment ask appropriate to the stage.
3. Rewrite any leading, yes/no, or hypothetical question. Show the before → after.
4. Include follow-up prompts: "Can you give me an example?", "Why was that hard?", "What did you do next?"

## Recruiting
Draft short, honest requests (use `outreach-drafts`): specific segment, why them, ask for 20–30 min, no pitch. Suggest channels where the segment already gathers.

## Synthesis (after 5+ interviews)
For each interview capture: segment, trigger, current solution, pains with **verbatim quotes**, spend/effort, commitment given. Then cluster across interviews: `pattern | # interviews (of N) | representative quotes | implication`. Report what **disconfirms** the hypotheses as prominently as what confirms them. With fewer than ~5 interviews per segment, call patterns tentative.
