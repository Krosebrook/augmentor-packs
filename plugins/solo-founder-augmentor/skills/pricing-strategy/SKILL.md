# Pricing Strategy

## Opinions
- Early founders usually **underprice**. Low prices attract price-sensitive customers and starve the business.
- Price from **value and alternatives**, sanity-check with **costs**, and never anchor only on a competitor's sticker.
- Pricing is a hypothesis — test it with real buying conversations, not surveys alone.

## Work through
1. **Value:** What is the outcome worth to the customer (money saved/made, hours × loaded rate, risk avoided)? Use the founder's customer evidence; label estimates.
2. **Alternatives' cost:** What do they pay now (tools, people, agency, the cost of doing nothing)?
3. **Value metric:** What unit scales with the value they get (seats, usage, projects, revenue processed, locations)? Good metrics are easy to understand, predictable for the buyer, and grow with value.
4. **Model:** one-time, subscription, usage-based, retainer, productized service, hybrid. Match to how value is delivered.
5. **Tiers (if any):** 2–3 tiers differentiated by customer segment needs, not random feature gating. Make the recommended tier obvious.
6. **Unit economics check:** price vs. cost to serve (including founder time at a realistic hourly value), payment fees, refunds; gross margin; for subscriptions, rough payback of acquisition cost. Use only the founder's numbers.
7. **Discount & terms policy:** annual prepay discount, pilot pricing with a set conversion date, never discount without getting something (term length, case study, referral).

## Testing price
- In sales conversations: state the price confidently and watch reactions; track close rate by price point.
- Survey methods like the Van Westendorp price sensitivity questions (too cheap / bargain / getting expensive / too expensive) can map a range — treat as directional, since stated intent ≠ purchase.
- If almost nobody objects to price, it's likely too low.

## Output
Pricing one-pager: value metric, model, price points with rationale, unit economics table, discount policy, and a 2-week test plan with the metric that decides.
