# Offer & Positioning

Positioning is context-setting: it tells the right customer why you're the obvious choice versus what they'd do otherwise.

## Work through (one block at a time, founder answers)
Inspired by April Dunford's positioning components (*Obviously Awesome*):
1. **Competitive alternatives:** What would your best customers do if you didn't exist? (Often a spreadsheet, an agency, an intern, "do nothing" — not just named competitors.)
2. **Unique attributes:** What do you have that those alternatives don't? Capabilities, not adjectives.
3. **Value:** What does each attribute enable for the customer? Push to an outcome they'd pay for (time, money, risk, status). Ask: "How do you know? Which customer said or showed this?"
4. **Best-fit customers:** Who cares a lot about that value? Firmographics/situation + the trigger moment that makes them look for a solution.
5. **Market category:** The frame that makes your value obvious. Existing category, a subsegment of one, or (rarely, expensive) a new one.

## Evidence discipline
Tag every claim: **[evidence: source]** (customer quote, sales data, retention) or **[hypothesis]**. Hypotheses go to `customer-discovery` for testing. Never fabricate market sizes, stats, or competitor facts — if a number is needed, give the founder a method to estimate it (bottom-up: # reachable customers × price × realistic conversion).

## The offer
Define: who it's for (and explicitly not for), the outcome promised, what's included, time to first value, price/terms (see `pricing-strategy`), risk reversal (guarantee/pilot), and the next step to buy.

## Output
- Positioning canvas (the five components with evidence tags).
- One-sentence positioning: "For [best-fit customer] who [trigger/pain], [product] is a [category] that [key value], unlike [main alternative]."
- Offer sheet (bullets).
- The 3 riskiest hypotheses and how to test each this week.

Push back on "for everyone," feature lists without outcomes, and copying competitors' words.
