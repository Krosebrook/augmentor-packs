# SOP & Delegation

## Step 1 — What to delegate (audit)
Have the founder list recurring tasks from the last 2 weeks with rough hours. Score each: **skill needed** (only me / trainable), **frequency**, **energy** (drains/neutral/energizes), **risk if done wrong**. Delegate first: frequent, trainable, draining, low-risk. Keep: strategy, key customer relationships, and anything that's the core differentiator — for now.

## Step 2 — The delegation brief
```
Task / outcome: what "done" looks like (with an example of good output)
Why it matters: context so they can make judgment calls
Inputs & access: tools, logins via a password manager share (never in the doc), files
Steps: link to SOP
Decision rights: what they can decide alone / must ask about / must never do
Quality checks: how they verify before handing back
Deadline & cadence; time budget
Communication: where to ask questions, expected response time
```

## Step 3 — The SOP
Capture by recording yourself doing the task once (screen recording + narration) or let Claude interview you. Then convert to steps: one action per step, starts with a verb, exact tool/field names, warnings before risky steps, decision rules for judgment calls, "done when" criteria, and troubleshooting. Use `[CONFIRM]` for anything unclear — don't invent tool names or settings.

## Step 4 — Hand-off protocol
1. They watch you / read the SOP. 2. They do it while you watch or review closely. 3. They do it; you check output against the quality checklist. 4. They do it; you spot-check. They update the SOP when something is unclear — the SOP gets better with every run.

## Contractor scope (if hiring)
Scope of work, deliverables, timeline, budget, ownership of work product, confidentiality — and suggest a proper contractor agreement (not legal advice; use a lawyer or a reputable template for their jurisdiction). Suggest a small paid trial task before a larger engagement.
