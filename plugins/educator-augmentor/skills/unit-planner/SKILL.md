# Unit Planner (Backward Design)

Follows Understanding by Design-style backward design (Wiggins & McTighe): decide the destination, define the evidence, then plan the route.

## Intake
Course/grade, standards (exact text supplied by the teacher — never supplied from memory), unit length in class periods and period length, required texts/curriculum, calendar constraints (holidays, testing days, assemblies), prior and next units.

## Stage 1 — Desired results
- **Standards** (quoted).
- **Enduring understandings** (2–4): big transferable ideas stated as full sentences ("Students will understand that…").
- **Essential questions** (2–4): open, arguable, recurring.
- **Knowledge & skills:** what students will know and be able to do.

## Stage 2 — Evidence
- **Summative performance task:** realistic, aligned to the understandings (offer a GRASPS-style frame: Goal, Role, Audience, Situation, Product, Standards). Rubric via `rubric-generator`.
- **Other evidence:** quizzes, checkpoints, exit tickets.
- Alignment check: every standard appears in at least one assessment; nothing is assessed that isn't taught.

## Stage 3 — Learning plan
- Week-by-week (or day-by-day) table: `day | objective | key activity | formative check | homework/materials`.
- Sequence from foundational knowledge → application → transfer; spiral the essential questions.
- Build in **formative checkpoints** before the summative, and **1 flex day per ~2 weeks** for reteaching or overflow.
- Mark where differentiation is planned (see `differentiation-udl`).

## Output
Unit overview (one page: standards, understandings, EQs, summative task), alignment matrix (`standard × lesson × assessment`), the calendar table, and a materials/prep list. Flag any standard that doesn't fit in the time available and ask the teacher what to compress.
