# Rubric Generator

## Intake
Assignment description, objectives/standards (teacher-supplied text), grade level, rubric type preference, number of levels, point weighting, and whether it will be used for feedback, grading, or both.

## Choose a type
- **Analytic** (criteria × levels): best for grading consistency and detailed feedback.
- **Single-point:** criteria with only the "proficient" description, plus space for "areas for growth" and "evidence of exceeding" — great for feedback and less overwhelming for students.
- **Holistic:** one overall score — fast, best for quick checks, weakest for feedback.

## Writing rules
1. **3–6 criteria** tied directly to objectives. Don't grade what you didn't teach. Keep mechanics/conventions to one criterion unless it's the target skill.
2. **Describe observable qualities**, not judgments: "Explains how each piece of evidence supports the claim" beats "Good analysis."
3. **Levels differ in quality, not quantity** where possible ("uses 3 sources" vs "uses 2" is weak; "sources are relevant and synthesized" vs "listed without connection" is better).
4. **Parallel language across levels** — same dimension, varying degree — so students can see what moving up looks like.
5. **Avoid vague frequency words alone** ("sometimes", "often") without a referent.
6. **Weight criteria** to reflect importance; show the math.
7. **Student-friendly version:** "I can…" language for the proficient level.

## Quality check before handing over
- Could two teachers score the same work within one level of each other? Flag criteria likely to cause disagreement.
- Does a top score require meeting the objective (not just effort/compliance)?
- Are non-academic factors (neatness, on-time) separated from mastery if the teacher's grading policy requires?

Offer to calibrate: the teacher scores 2–3 anonymized samples and you highlight where the descriptors were ambiguous.
