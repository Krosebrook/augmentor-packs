# Differentiation & UDL

## Principles
- **Keep the goal, flex the path.** Change access, support, and expression — not the standard — unless an IEP specifies modified goals.
- **Design up front (UDL) before retrofitting.** CAST's UDL framework organizes options around three principles: multiple means of **engagement**, **representation**, and **action & expression**. (CAST publishes the current UDL Guidelines at udlguidelines.cast.org — refer the teacher there for checkpoint detail.)
- **IEP/504 accommodations are legal requirements, not suggestions.** Ask the teacher what's specified; don't guess or diagnose.
- **Privacy:** work from needs descriptions, not student names or records.

## Workflow
1. Get the lesson/task and the learner profile in needs terms (e.g., "3 students reading ~2 grade levels below", "2 newcomers, Spanish L1", "one student needs extended time + text-to-speech").
2. **UDL pass** on the base lesson — suggest 1–2 changes per principle that help everyone:
   - Engagement: choice in topic/context, relevance, clear goals, chunked tasks with progress checks.
   - Representation: text + visual + audio/model; pre-taught vocabulary; worked examples; graphic organizers.
   - Action & expression: options to show learning (written, oral, diagram), sentence frames, tools (speech-to-text, calculators where the skill isn't the target).
3. **Tiered supports** for the same task:
   - **Support:** scaffolds (frames, partially completed examples, word banks, chunked directions, small-group reteach).
   - **Core:** the task as designed.
   - **Extension:** more depth/complexity (transfer, justify, create), not more volume.
4. **Multilingual learners:** language objective alongside the content objective; sentence frames by proficiency level; visuals; strategic L1 use; structured talk before writing.
5. **Check:** does every version still assess the same objective?

## Output
A table: `lesson part | base | UDL tweak | support tier | extension tier`, plus a list of materials to prepare and a reminder to cross-check against each student's documented accommodations.
