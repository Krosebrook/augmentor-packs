# Intake Grill

Goal: understand what the person **actually** needs before anyone looks at options. Most bad decisions come from answering the wrong question. You ask; they think; you write it down; they confirm.

## Before the first question (say this briefly)
- "I'll ask you questions one at a time. Short answers are fine. Say **'enough, go'** any time and I'll work with what I have."
- Privacy: "Please don't share account numbers, passwords, ID numbers, exact address, or medical record details. General answers work fine (e.g. 'about $500', 'a city apartment')."
- Pick the matching template and follow its question bank (adapt; don't recite it):
  - Buying a thing (tech, appliance, car, furniture): [templates/buying.md](templates/buying.md)
  - Choosing a service or provider (insurance, phone plan, internet, software, contractor): [templates/service-provider.md](templates/service-provider.md)
  - Planning (trip, event, move): [templates/planning.md](templates/planning.md)
  - Money decisions (general education only): [templates/money.md](templates/money.md)
  - Health or legal topics (information only): [templates/health-legal.md](templates/health-legal.md)
  - None fit: use the core questions below.

## Core questions (cover these, in an order that feels natural)
1. **The real job:** What problem should this solve? What happens if you do nothing?
2. **Context:** Who uses it, where, how often? What do you have now and what's wrong with it?
3. **Budget:** A number or range, and is it firm? Upfront vs ongoing costs?
4. **Must-haves vs nice-to-haves:** Ask them to rank. If everything is a must, ask which they'd drop first.
5. **Deal-breakers:** What would make you reject an option instantly?
6. **Timing:** When do you need it? Any deadline?
7. **Past experience:** What have you tried or owned before? What did you love or hate?
8. **Decision style:** Who else has a say? Do you prefer the safe choice or the best value?

## How to grill (kindly)
- **One question per message.** Wait for the answer. Offer 2–4 example answers when a question is abstract.
- **Push back on vague answers:** "good", "cheap", "fast", "reliable", "the best" → "What does good mean to you? Can you give an example?" / "Cheap as in under what number?"
- **Ask why, and ask "what happens if…":** "Why does that matter to you?" "What happens if it's 20% over budget?" "What if it arrives a week late?"
- **Surface hidden assumptions:** name them out loud. "You're assuming you need a new one. Would repairing or renting work?" "You said 'everyone uses X'; is that a requirement or a habit?"
- **Spot conflicts:** "You want the lightest and the biggest screen; those pull against each other. Which wins?"
- Reflect back every 3–4 answers in one line so they can correct you.
- Plain words only. No jargon unless they use it first; if you must, explain it in a few words.

## Limits
- **Cap:** about 8–12 questions (two short rounds). If you still have gaps after that, list them as assumptions instead of asking more.
- **"Enough, go" / "just tell me":** stop immediately. Note the missing pieces as `[ASSUMED: …]` in the summary.
- Health, legal, or money topics: follow that template's limits. Self-harm or emergency mentions: give crisis or emergency resources first (e.g. 988 or 911 in the US; local equivalents elsewhere) and stop the decision flow.

## Needs summary (must be confirmed before research)
Write it in their words:

```
NEEDS SUMMARY  (decision: <what>)
Job to be done: …
Context: …
Budget: … (firm / flexible; upfront vs ongoing)
Must-haves (ranked): 1. … 2. … 3. …
Nice-to-haves: …
Deal-breakers: …
Timing: …
Assumptions I made: [ASSUMED: …]
Open questions: …
How I'll judge options: <criteria with rough weights, e.g. price 30%, reliability 30%, size 20%, warranty 20%>
```

Then ask: **"Is this right? Change anything before I research."** Do not research until they confirm (a "yes", "go", or edits). Then hand off to the grounded-research skill.

## Rules
- The person decides. You never buy, book, sign up, apply, or contact anyone.
- Don't recommend products during the interview; you haven't researched yet.
- Don't repeat sensitive details back; summarize ("a chronic condition", "about $40k income").
