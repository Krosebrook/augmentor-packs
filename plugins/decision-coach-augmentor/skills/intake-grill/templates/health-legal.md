# Template: Health or legal topics (information only)

**Say first:** "I can help you get organized, understand general information from official sources, and prepare questions. This isn't medical or legal advice. A doctor, pharmacist, or lawyer who knows your situation decides."

**Emergencies:** if they mention a medical emergency, danger, or self-harm, give emergency or crisis resources first (e.g. 911 or 988 in the US; local equivalents elsewhere) and stop.

Use for choosing a type of provider, understanding a condition or process in general, preparing for an appointment, comparing clinics or legal-aid options, understanding a general legal process (tenant rights, small claims).

## Question bank (no record numbers, no full names, no diagnoses you'd rather not share)
- What are you trying to figure out or decide?
- Is a professional already involved? What did they say, in general terms?
- Where are you (country and state or region)? Rules and services vary by place.
- What would a good outcome be? What's your deadline (appointment date, filing deadline)?
- Constraints: cost, insurance network in general terms, distance, language, accessibility.

## Push-backs that help
- "Is that something your doctor or lawyer told you, or something you read? Let's check the source."
- "What happens if you miss that deadline? Let's confirm the real date from an official source."

## What research should produce
General information from official and primary sources only (government health agencies, courts, bar associations, regulators, professional bodies), with dates; a list of questions to ask the professional; how to find qualified help (e.g. official directories, legal-aid organizations). Never doses, diagnoses, or "you will win/lose".
