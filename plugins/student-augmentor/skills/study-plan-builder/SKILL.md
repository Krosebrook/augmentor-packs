# Study Plan Builder

Turn a syllabus into a plan the student will actually follow. Realism beats ambition.

## Inputs to gather (ask for anything missing — never invent dates)
- The syllabus or a list of: assessments (type, date, weight), weekly readings/topics.
- Hard constraints: work shifts, other courses, commute, sleep.
- Honest available hours per weekday/weekend day.
- Current standing: what feels shaky, past grades if they want to share.

If the syllabus is a file, read it. Extract every dated item into a table first and ask the student to confirm it before planning. Flag anything ambiguous ("Week 9 quiz — no date given").

## Planning rules (opinionated defaults; adjust with the student)
1. **Weight by stakes × weakness.** Allocate time in proportion to grade weight, then boost topics the student rated weak.
2. **Spaced, not massed.** Each topic gets a first-learn session plus reviews at roughly +1 day, +3–4 days, +1 week, +2 weeks. Cramming the night before is a review, not a learn.
3. **Retrieval over rereading.** Every session has an active output: practice problems, self-quiz, blurting, or teaching it aloud. "Reread chapter 4" is not a valid task.
4. **Buffer 20%.** Leave one unscheduled block per week. Plans without slack fail on the first sick day.
5. **Exam runway.** Start dedicated exam prep 7–10 days before high-weight exams, with at least one timed practice session.
6. **Sessions are 25–50 minutes** with a specific deliverable ("Finish 6 problems from 3.2, check against answer key").
7. **Never schedule more than the student said they have.** If it doesn't fit, show the overflow and make them choose what to cut or deprioritize.

## Output
1. **Deadline table** — date | item | weight | prep start date.
2. **Week-by-week plan** — for each week: focus topics, sessions (day, duration, task, type: learn/review/practice/exam-prep).
3. **Risk list** — crunch weeks where deadlines collide, with a mitigation for each.
4. **Weekly check-in prompt** — 3 questions the student answers each Sunday to adjust the plan (what slipped, what's shaky, what's next).

Offer to export the plan as a CSV the student can import into their calendar or task app.
