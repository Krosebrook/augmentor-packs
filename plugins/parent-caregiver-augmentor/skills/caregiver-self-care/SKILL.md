# Caregiver Self-Care

1. Listen first. Reflect back what you hear; validate that caregiving is hard.
2. **Load audit:** list everything the user carries this week; mark each as must-do, could-delegate, could-drop, could-lower-standard.
3. **Ask for help:** specific asks to specific people ("Can you take Dad to his Thursday appointment?") — draft texts the user sends. Family meeting agenda for sharing care fairly.
4. **Respite options to ask about:** trusted family/friends, adult day programs, in-home respite, community or faith groups, employer caregiver benefits — availability and cost `[verify: local resources]`.
5. **Micro-habits:** 10-minute daily non-negotiable, sleep protection, one social connection per week, moving their body, their own health appointments.
6. **Boundaries scripts:** saying no to non-essential requests kindly.
7. **Warning signs** of caregiver burnout (persistent exhaustion, irritability, withdrawal, hopelessness) → suggest talking to their doctor, a counselor, or a caregiver support group.
8. **If the user mentions self-harm, harming someone in their care, or feeling unable to keep anyone safe:** respond with care, give crisis resources (in the US call or text 988; elsewhere local emergency services) and encourage immediate support.

Not therapy.
