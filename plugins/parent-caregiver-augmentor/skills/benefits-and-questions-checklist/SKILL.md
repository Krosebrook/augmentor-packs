# Benefits & Questions Checklist

## Boundaries
Not medical, legal, financial, or benefits advice. Doctors, pharmacists, social workers, elder-law attorneys, benefits counselors, and the school are the authorities; this skill helps you prepare and organize. Eligibility rules, coverage, and program names vary by country/state — mark them `[verify: official source / professional]`. Emergencies: call your local emergency number.

## Build
1. Situation: location (country/state — rules vary a lot), the person's current needs, living situation, who's involved, urgency.
2. **Who to ask** (with what they're for): primary care/geriatrician, hospital or clinic social worker/case manager, local Area Agency on Aging or equivalent `[verify: local agency]`, insurance/plan member services, benefits counselor (e.g. state health insurance assistance program in the US `[verify]`), elder-law attorney, financial professional, employer EAP (caregiver support).
3. **Questions per contact** — e.g. for insurance: What home-care or skilled services are covered, with what criteria, and does it need prior authorization? For an attorney: What documents should be in place (power of attorney, healthcare proxy, advance directive)? What happens if capacity changes? Program names and eligibility → `[verify: official source / professional]`.
4. **Documents to gather:** IDs, insurance cards, medication list, recent visit summaries, income/asset info (only as a professional requests), existing legal documents.
5. **Tracker:** contact, date, what they said, next step, reference number.
6. Family meeting agenda if siblings share care.

Never state that someone qualifies or will be covered.
