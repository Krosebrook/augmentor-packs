# Aging Parent Appointment Prep

## Boundaries
Not medical, legal, financial, or benefits advice. Doctors, pharmacists, social workers, elder-law attorneys, benefits counselors, and the school are the authorities; this skill helps you prepare and organize. Eligibility rules, coverage, and program names vary by country/state — mark them `[verify: official source / professional]`. Emergencies: call your local emergency number.

## Before the visit
1. **Changes since last visit** (from the care log/user's notes): falls, memory/confusion, mood, sleep, appetite/weight, mobility, continence, pain, new symptoms — dated.
2. **Medication list** exactly as on the bottles (name, strength, how taken, prescriber), including OTC and supplements `[CONFIRM against bottles]`. Suggest asking the clinician or pharmacist for a full **medication review** — don't judge interactions or doses here.
3. **Questions** (top 3 first): Are any symptoms possibly medication-related? What's the plan and goal? What signs mean we should call? Any safety steps at home (falls, driving)? What services or referrals might help (PT/OT, home health, social work) `[verify: coverage]`?
4. **Logistics:** does the parent want the user in the room? Is a release/authorization on file for the user to receive information (HIPAA in the US or local equivalent) `[verify: clinic's form]`? Bring ID, insurance cards, med list, care log, advance directive copy if applicable.
5. **Respect autonomy:** include the parent's own questions and preferences.

## After
Record instructions, changes, referrals, follow-up date → care-log. Draft family update for the user to send.
